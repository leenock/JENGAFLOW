Yes. I would add **site progress documentation** as a core part of the business flow, rather than treating photos and videos as an afterthought.

### Updated Business Flow

The Construction Site Tracker begins when a contractor or construction company creates an account on the platform. The company owner becomes the main account owner and can then set up the organisation and create one or more construction projects. Each project contains important information such as the project name, location, client, contract value, planned budget and start date. The project becomes the central place where all financial, operational and progress information related to the construction site is recorded.

Once a project has been created, the owner can invite clerks or foremen who will help manage the day-to-day activities on the site. These users are assigned to specific projects and are responsible for recording materials, labour payments, deliveries and other site activities. The owner can also register the vehicles used on the project, including company-owned and hired vehicles, so that deliveries can be properly tracked.

During the daily operation of the construction site, the clerk or foreman can record what is happening on the site directly from their phone. When materials are purchased or used, they record the material, quantity, unit price, supplier and date. When workers are paid, the labour payment is recorded with the worker or team name, role, amount, payment type and date. When a delivery arrives, the clerk can record the materials delivered, quantity, delivery fee, date, receiving person and the vehicle that brought the materials. This creates a clear connection between **what was delivered, when it arrived and which vehicle transported it**.

The platform will also include a **Site Progress and Media** section where the site team can upload photos and videos showing the current progress of the construction project. For example, the foreman can take photos of the building every day or at important stages of construction and upload them to the project. These updates can include a short description such as *"Foundation completed — Block A"* or *"First-floor columns completed."* The system records the date, time and person who uploaded the update, creating a visual timeline of the project's development.

This progress timeline allows the owner to look back at the project and see how the building has developed over time. Instead of relying only on written reports, the owner can visually compare the construction site from one day or stage to another. Photos and videos can therefore serve as a **digital construction diary**, providing evidence of progress and helping the owner understand what has actually been completed on site.

Progress updates can also be connected to daily site activities. For example, if a delivery of 100 bags of cement arrives on Monday, the clerk can record the delivery and upload photos showing the materials arriving at the site. If construction reaches the first-floor slab later in the week, the team can upload photos or videos showing that progress. This creates a relationship between the project's **financial records, deliveries and physical construction progress**.

As activities are recorded, the system continuously builds the project's history. Every material purchase, labour payment, delivery, vehicle movement and progress update becomes part of the project record. The owner can therefore see not only how much money has been spent, but also **what has physically happened on the construction site**.

The system then uses the recorded financial information to calculate the project's financial position. Material costs, labour payments and delivery costs are combined to determine the total amount spent. The system compares this spending against the project's budget and contract value, allowing the owner to see whether the project is remaining within its expected costs and what the current running profit or loss looks like.

The owner dashboard can therefore provide both a **financial view and a construction progress view**. The financial side can show the contract value, budget, total spending, material costs, labour costs, delivery costs, running profit and profit margin. The progress side can show the latest photos and videos, recent progress updates, recent deliveries and the project's activity timeline.

The clerk or foreman will have a much simpler interface focused on capturing information from the construction site. They can quickly add materials, labour payments, deliveries, vehicle information and progress photos or videos. They will not have access to sensitive financial information such as the project's profit or profit margin.

Over time, the system becomes a complete **digital record of the construction project**. The owner can see how the project started, what was delivered, how much was spent, who recorded each activity and how the building physically progressed from one stage to another. At the end of a reporting period, the owner can also export financial and operational records for internal reporting, billing support or project documentation.

### The core idea becomes:

**Create Project → Set Contract & Budget → Add Team → Register Vehicles → Record Daily Activities → Record Deliveries → Upload Progress Photos/Videos → Track Spending → Monitor Budget → Monitor Construction Progress → View Running Profit → Export Project Records**

I think the **photo/video progress timeline is a very good addition**, because it differentiates the system from simply being an expense tracker. It makes it more like a **construction project monitoring system** where the owner can answer two questions from one place:

> **"How much have we spent?"**

and

> **"How far has the construction actually reached?"**
